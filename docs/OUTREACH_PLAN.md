# GovLens AI — Manual Outreach Plan (Track B)

Companion to `EXECUTION_PLAN.md`'s "Track B." This covers who we're targeting, when to actually send outreach, where leads come from, and who does what.

## Who we're targeting (ICP)

Straight from `PROJECT_BRIEF.md` — don't drift from this without a reason:
- US-based small businesses, **1–50 employees**.
- SAM.gov-registered or eligible.
- Especially valuable: holders of **8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB** certifications — these are explicit legal set-aside categories, so a certified business has a structural reason to care about federal contracting right now.
- Industries: IT services, professional services, construction, light manufacturing (per the original plan) — but any small business that's SAM.gov-eligible and NAICS-classified is fair game.
- Decision-maker titles: Owner, President, CEO, Founder, Principal, VP Business Development — at this company size, it's almost always the owner/founder making this call, not a mid-level employee.

## When to actually send outreach (research, not guesswork)

Pulled from multiple independent 2026 studies (Snov.io, Instantly.ai, Saleshandy, Belkins, HubSpot, and LinkedIn's own internal data). Where sources disagreed, the majority consensus is used.

### Cold email
- **Best days: Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday.** Every source agrees mid-week beats the edges of the week. Wednesday and Thursday post the highest reply rates in most studies; one 2026 source ranks Tuesday first. Practically: **any of Tue/Wed/Thu is fine, don't send on Monday or Friday.**
- **Best time: mid-morning, recipient's local time — roughly 9:30–11:30 AM.** Reply rates peak in the morning (7–11 AM window) even though open rates sometimes peak slightly later (12–4 PM) — optimize for replies, not opens.
- **Avoid:** Monday (lowest open *and* reply rates across every study), Friday afternoons, and late evenings.
- **Baseline expectations, so nobody panics at "low" numbers:** typical cold email reply rates sit at **1–5%**. Even a "good" campaign rarely clears high single digits. 1000 sends producing 20–50 real replies is a normal, healthy result — not a failure.

Sources: [Best Time to Send Cold Emails in 2026 — Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/best-time-to-send-cold-emails) · [Cold Email Statistics 2026 — Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/cold-email-statistics/) · [B2B Cold Email Response Rates 2026 Study — Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates) · [Best Weekday for B2B Reply Rates 2026 — Growleads](https://growleads.io/blog/best-cold-email-timing-to-get-high-responses/) · [Best Days to Send Cold Emails — Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/best-days-to-send-cold-emails)

### LinkedIn (connection requests + InMail)
- **Best days: Tuesday–Thursday**, same mid-week pattern as email. Tuesday specifically leads on response rate in most studies (6.9–18.5% range).
- **Best time: 10–11 AM, or 5–6 PM, recipient's local time.**
- **Genuinely conflicting data point, worth knowing:** one study (AuthoredUp) found Sunday sends get the *highest acceptance rate* for connection requests specifically — the theory is less competition in a quieter feed. If mid-week volume feels saturated, Sunday is worth an A/B test, not just a rule to ignore.
- **LinkedIn's own research says InMail timing barely matters** — any time of day gets a similar response rate. Personalization and correctly targeting the right person matter far more than timing for InMail specifically. Don't over-optimize the clock for InMail; do over-optimize who you're sending it to.
- **InMail response rates (10–25%) are dramatically higher than cold email (1–5%).** If forced to pick one channel to prioritize, LinkedIn outreach to the right decision-maker beats cold email on pure response rate, even though email is easier to scale to volume.

Sources: [LinkedIn Outreach Benchmarks 2026 — Overloop](https://overloop.com/blog/linkedin-outreach-benchmarks) · [LinkedIn Response Rates by Day — Closely](https://blog.closelyhq.com/linkedin-response-rates-by-day-best-time-send-messages/) · [Data Reveals the Best Time to Send InMails — LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/best-time-to-send-inmails) · [LinkedIn Response Rate Benchmarks 2026 — LeadSpark AI](https://www.leadspark-ai.com/resources/linkedin-response-rate-benchmarks)

### Practical schedule
Send batches Tuesday–Thursday, mid-morning (your recipient's timezone — most targets are US-based, so plan around US business hours). Don't send on Monday or Friday. If LinkedIn connection volume plateaus, test a Sunday batch against a Tuesday batch and compare acceptance rates directly rather than assuming either is right.

## Lead sourcing: Apollo free tier — read this before planning around 1000 leads

**Important constraint, verified before writing this plan:** Apollo's free plan caps **CSV export at roughly 10 contacts per month** ([Apollo.io Pricing 2026 — Salesmotion](https://salesmotion.io/blog/apollo-pricing), [Apollo.io Pricing Breakdown 2026 — Cotera](https://cotera.co/articles/apollo-io-pricing-guide)). That's nowhere near enough to export 1000 leads to a spreadsheet in a month on the free tier. Two ways to actually hit 1000:

1. **Don't export — outreach from inside Apollo directly.** Apollo lets you build a saved list from a filtered search and run email sequences *from within the platform* without exporting to CSV. This isn't subject to the same export-credit cap. If you're comfortable running the actual sending through Apollo's own sequence tool (or connecting your email to it), this is the realistic path to real volume on the free tier.
2. **Spread it across months, or upgrade.** If you specifically need the raw contact list exported (e.g., to use a different sending tool), the free tier realistically gets you ~10–25/month depending on current terms — hitting 1000 that way would take most of a year. A low-cost paid Apollo tier removes this ceiling if budget allows; check current pricing before committing, since Apollo's tiers change.

**Recommendation:** build the list and send from inside Apollo (option 1) for email volume; use Apollo's search purely for LinkedIn lead discovery (find the person, then connect/message on LinkedIn directly, which has no Apollo export limit at all since you're not exporting anything).

## Suggested Apollo search filters (for building the list/sequence)

- **Company headcount:** 1–50 employees.
- **Location:** United States (all states, or start with a few states if you want a smaller first batch to test messaging).
- **Keywords (company description / industry):** try combinations of `"government contractor"`, `"federal contracting"`, `"SDVOSB"`, `"WOSB"`, `"HUBZone"`, `"8(a) certified"`, `"SAM registered"` — these terms show up in company profiles/descriptions for businesses already positioned toward federal work, which is a much stronger signal than generic industry filtering alone.
- **Industry (fallback if keyword search is too narrow):** IT Services, Construction, Professional Services, Manufacturing — matches the ICP in the product brief.
- **Titles:** Owner, President, CEO, Founder, Principal, Managing Director, VP Business Development.
- **Technology/other filters:** not especially useful for this ICP — skip unless a specific pattern emerges after the first batch.

Start with the keyword-based search (SDVOSB/WOSB/HUBZone/8(a) terms) — it's a much higher-intent list than a broad "small business, 1-50 employees" filter, even though it'll return fewer total results. Quality over 1000 for its own sake.

## Who does what (founder + partner)

- **Founder:** owns the actual outreach copy/messaging (knows the product and the pitch best), owns LinkedIn outreach personally (LinkedIn profile continuity matters — better if it's the founder's own network/voice), tracks replies and conversion into pilot conversations.
- **Partner:** owns Apollo list-building and filter iteration (a good task alongside his TypeScript ramp-up, and doesn't require deep product knowledge to execute well), can help draft/test email sequence variants once the founder has approved a base template, tracks send-volume and open/reply metrics.
- **Both:** review response data weekly and adjust — if LinkedIn is meaningfully outperforming email (likely, per the research above), shift effort there rather than sticking to a 50/50 split by habit.

## What "done" looks like for this track

Not a fixed lead count — a **handful of real conversations that convert into actual paying pilot customers** (per `PROJECT_BRIEF.md`'s validation logic: if people won't pay for the manual version, they won't pay for the automated one). Track replies and conversions, not just sends. If outreach volume is high but conversion is zero after a few weeks, that's a signal to revisit the pitch or the ICP — not to just send more.
